Our Culinary Heritage & the Master Cooks of Tamil Nadu

Long before industrial mills and quick-service counters altered the cadence of Indian dining, the great tiffin halls of the South were anchored by the revered Karisamayal master cooks. These culinary artisans did not view cooking as a trade, but as an austere ritual dictated by seasonal rhythms, precise stone temperatures, and sacred proportions.

At Hotel New Moorthi Cafe, our kitchens safeguard these original manuscripts of taste. We reject artificial thickeners, synthetic colors, and pre-packaged masalas. Each podi is roasted in small batches, every sambar decoction follows an unhurried simmering arc, and our ferments rest in seasoned clay pots according to traditional air currents.

The Daily Symphony

A Day at Hotel New Moorthi Cafe

From the quiet crackle of the early dawn charcoal to late dusk conversations over roasted tiffin, our day moves to an ancient gastronomic pulse.

Dawn Phase wb_twilight

The First Decoction & Sizzle

Before first light, whole black mustard seeds sputter in earthen skillets. Heavy brass filter drums drip dark Chikmagalur brew drop by drop, filling the chilly morning corridor with intoxicating aroma.

Signature Morning Touch

Hot Mallipoo Idlis drenched in pure Ghee Guntur Podi.

Noon Phase wb_sunny

The Great Sambar Simmer

Split toor dal boils to silky tenderness with Madras shallots and country drumsticks. Freshly ground coriander, roasted fenugreek, and asafoetida temper the midday feast served over gleaming green leaves.

Signature Noon Touch

Claypot rasam enriched with freshly crushed heirloom cumin.

Dusk Phase bedtime

Tiffin & Twilight Conversations

The tawa sizzles incessantly with golden paper-thin ghee roasts and spiced medu vadas. Regulars gather for steaming tumblers of filter coffee, trading neighbourhood stories under ambient brass glow.

Signature Evening Touch

Crispy Rava Masala Dosa served with three hand-churred chutneys.
